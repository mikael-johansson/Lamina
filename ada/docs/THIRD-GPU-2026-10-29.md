# A third RTX 3090 for IQ3_S: estimates (2026-10-29)

What a 3rd card gives when only part of its VRAM (6 / 12 / 18 / 24 GB) is ours, which multi-GPU design to build
for it, and how many experts can stay on the CPU before it limits decode. Everything here is an *estimate* from
the routing trace, the pool timeline and earlier measurements; nothing was run on three cards.

## Inputs

- IQ3_S: 24,576 experts (48 layers x 512) at ~2 MiB each, ~512 per GiB.
- Production today (`--decode-split 23`, reserves 200/450): 10,858 main + 9,464 second slots, ~20,200 holding
  experts at ~30K context; ~4,400 experts left to the CPU. Logged hit rates on real traffic 96.7-97.3%.
- CPU cost: ~80 us of pool time per distinct missed expert per layer (`bench/results/cpu-pool/tl.out`: 6.7 ms
  pool for 1.68 distinct misses per layer). The GPU's own expert work is ~67 us per layer, so any layer with a miss
  waits on the CPU: wait = max(0, 80 d - 67) us per layer.
- Hit rates vs VRAM: `bench/miss_sim.py` replays the routing trace (`~/git/strata/bench/runs/trace-iq3`, not in the repo; 18 requests,
  2,032 windows, 3.35 rows per window) with static rankings: the learned profile, the shipped profile and
  leave-one-request-out frequencies.
  - Check against the old run: the model predicts ~4.3 ms CPU wait per window at that run's miss level; the
    timeline measured 4.1 ms.
  - Check against today: the learned profile at 20,200 resident gives 2.95% misses, production logs ~3%.
- K/V: ~11.6 MiB per 1K tokens across the cards (~6 expert slots per 1K tokens), consistent with the 1.5 GB a
  128K batch slot borrows.
- A 3rd-card share loses ~0.5 GiB to the CUDA context and scratch.

- C=2 today reaches 1.30-1.43x against a 1.92x bound (23/25 layers); the CPU pool holds it back. Without misses

- The card with the most layers sets the bound for C>=3:

| 3rd card | layers per card | bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 GB | ~5 / 21.5 / 21.5 | 2.2x |
| 12 GB | ~10 / 19 / 19 | 2.5x |
| 18 GB | ~15 / 16.5 / 16.5 | 2.9x |
| 24 GB | 16 / 16 / 16 | 3.0x |

- C=4 and C=8 stay flat: there are as many lanes as cards and further requests take turns (today C=4 288 vs C=2
  297). Verifying several requests in one window would lift that; the main card's per-layer work is mostly fixed
  kernel cost, so a second request in a window would cost well under 2x. Not built.
- C=8 also costs experts: eight ~30K slots take ~2.8 GiB of K/V. 6 GB has no spare (misses return); 12 GB covers
  short contexts, not long; 18/24 GB absorb it. Production caps at `parallel: 3` anyway.
- Needs: a 3-stage `--decode-split` (Phase 1 to three cards) for C=1/C=2; three requests in flight (Phase 2 from
  two lanes to three) for C>=3. Without that work the 3rd card only gives the C=1 gain and C=2 stays near ~330.

## Tensor parallel

TP gains less than on a dense model, and PCIe adds a tax without killing it:

- The main card is latency-bound: of its ~150 us per layer before the router, ~65 us is weight reading (halves
  with TP=2) and ~85 us fixed per-kernel cost (does not). The experts are tiny matmuls (640 wide, 10 quant
  blocks), already latency-bound, so splitting them saves little.
- TP=2 adds two exchanges per layer, ~96 per window, of ~70 KB at ~10-20 us each over P2P (measured 11-21 us
  one way, host-launched, 2026-10-26): ~1-2 ms per ~15 ms window, a 7-13% tax. An NVLink bridge (two cards only)
  would cut it to roughly a third. The layer split has one hand-off per window per card boundary, so PCIe latency is
  noise there.
- TP=3 is out: 2 KV heads, expert widths 640 / 2,560 that do not split three ways, and the x4 card would be the
  slowest member of every exchange.

| design | per layer | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TP=2 with today's misses | | ~+10-15% C=1: the CPU misses stay the longest piece |
| layer split, all experts in VRAM | ~236 us | |
| TP=2, all experts in VRAM | ~189 us | ~+20-25% C=1 over the 3-card layer split |

## A miss card instead of the CPU

The 3rd card holds only the cold experts and runs the misses. P2P works between the current two cards
(`nvidia-smi topo -p2p r`: OK). A miss after the router: rows out over the x4 link (~17 KB, ~10-20 us), 1-2
experts (~15-20 us), partial sums back and combine (~10-20 us): **~40-60 us round trip**, against ~80 us per missed
expert on the CPU. It has to be device-side: today's host-mediated second-card tier costs ~100 us per share.

| design (short tasks, tok/s total) | per layer | C=1 | C=2 | C=3+ | work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| today, 2-card split | | 229 | 297 | ~290 | |
| TP=2 for the hot experts + miss card | ~195-200 us | ~290-300 | ~300-330 | ~300-330 | weeks |
| 2-stage split + miss card | ~236-240 us | ~245-250 | ~385 | ~385 | days to a week |
| 3-stage split (12 GB third card) | ~236 us | ~250 | ~400 | ~500 | ~1 week+ |

- Under TP=2 the hot experts take ~35-40 us, so a miss is exposed for ~10-25 us in ~40% of layers (~5-10 us per
  layer on average). Under the layer split each stage keeps its ~67 us of local expert work and the round trip
  mostly hides beneath it.
- TP is one lane: C=2 stays flat unless requests share a window. It wins C=1 by ~18% and loses ~25% at C=2.
- 12 GB holds all ~4,400 cold experts plus ~1,000 spare for hot ones pushed off by K/V growth; 6 GB holds ~2,600
  and the rest go back to the CPU.

## A shared 3rd card

With 12 GB the 3rd card holds ~10 of the 48 layers against ~19 on each of the others, about half their work:

| | layers | busy at C=1 (13.4 ms window) | busy with three in flight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| card A | ~19 | ~4.5 ms (~35%) | ~80% (A or B is the bottleneck) |
| card B | ~19 | ~35% | ~80% |
| 3rd card, 12 GB | ~10 | ~2.4 ms (~18%) | ~40-45% |

It has time left for another tenant, but that is not what decides the cost. Without MPS, CUDA time-slices between
processes (slices of about a millisecond or more): if the other tenant is busy when our block arrives, the window
waits, since at C=1 the stage is on the critical path. An idle tenant (a model loaded but mostly idle) costs
nothing. MPS would let both share the card's compute units at once; unconfirmed on these GeForce cards.

A 3-stage split meets the shared card once per window, as one block. A miss card meets it in each of the ~20
layers per window with a miss, each one on the critical path. So the 3-stage split is both the better design and
the safer one on a shared card. Make the 3rd card a middle stage without the output head and MTP to keep it light.

## Recommendation

1. Build the 3-stage split, with the 12 GB card as a light middle stage of ~10 layers.
2. The miss card only as an interim step if the 3-stage work looks too big.
3. TP=2 only if C=1 is the last thing left to push, and after fusing kernels: the fixed ~85 us per layer is the
   biggest single C=1 item, and fusing helps every design.

When the riser arrives, measure:

1. P2P to the x4 slot on the CPU lanes: whether it works and how long a hand-off takes (the miss-card numbers
   assume ~40-60 us round trip, not ~100).
2. Our stage beside the other tenant's real load: window times and their long-tail stalls.
3. Whether MPS works on these cards.
